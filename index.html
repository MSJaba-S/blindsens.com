The problem

Feel finds the sens you're used to.

Every sens finder asks how it feels. But after a few thousand hours, your current sens always feels right. That's habit, not performance. To find out what you actually aim best with, you have to remove the one thing that biases the answer: knowing which sens you're on.

How it works

Three steps. One honest answer.

01

Play blind

Short aim blocks at five hidden sensitivities, from ×0.70 to ×1.42 of your own. Shuffled. You never know which one you're on.

02

The data decides

Every block becomes one data point. A curve is fitted through all of them, resampled 400 times and tested before anything is recommended.

03

One number, every game

Your result in cm/360°, converted to 39 FPS games, with sens per hero where the game has heroes.

The method

Where you actually perform best.

No black box. The same statistics researchers use to find an optimum, in a test that takes about ten minutes.

1

Fit a curve through every block, one point per block.

2

Resample 400 times to see how far the peak can move.

3

Test the curvature. The peak has to be real (t below −2.0), not noise.

4

Check how you move. Overshoot, direction changes and lead or lag give a second, independent signal.

5

Only then recommend a change, with a proven range.

Five disciplines

Tested the way you actually play.

Flick

One target at a time, big jumps.

Tracking

Hold your aim on a smoothly moving target.

Reactive tracking

It strafes and jumps, like in hero shooters.

Target switch

Several targets. Switch fast.

Micro adjust

Small targets, close together.

Pick a role (all-round, entry/flank, sharpshooter, anchor or support) and the test uses the disciplines that matter for it. Every test adds to the same profile, so the answer gets sharper over time.
